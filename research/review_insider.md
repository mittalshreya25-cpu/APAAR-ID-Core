## Objections and resolution

The principal challenge is the **day-level date inference**, not the staged framework. B and C visibly carry a 13 November 2025 masthead and a 14 November electronic code. D itself demonstrates that issue date and electronic code can differ. A code alone does not establish the legally operative publication date. INSIDER-1's cautious wording should become an explicit unresolved day-level calculation in the final synthesis; the safe research output is months and elapsed-period text.

The 2026 order exists in the original official PDF. I inspected its English operative paragraphs: it substitutes the child/disability parallel wording and the data-audit wording. Its own commencement upon publication does not accelerate the underlying substantive provisions. This rebuts both the assumption that the order is merely a secondary report and the assumption that it changes all DPDP dates.

The narrow child-education exception survives attempted falsification against the actual Fourth Schedule: it cannot support broad employer disclosure. The retention and two breach-reporting regimes also survive, provided future DPDP duties and existing CERT-In directions remain clearly separated. No verified source here establishes the production platform's entity status, legal ground or compliance.
