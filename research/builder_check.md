# Builder check — 28 October 2026

Bounded review of `PROJECT_SPEC.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `CONTEXT.md` and `PHASES_AND_TASKS.md`; no external research or implementation. Ranked questions where competing reasonable interpretations change the product or its safety. Live-registry, regulatory-evidence and production-assurance gates are intentionally excluded.

1. **High — What is the current reported population, independently of the certified population?** The specification preserves invalid/excess submissions for regulator inspection (Finding 3), but gives only certified replacements an effective pointer and tells the model to count effective snapshots (Data model; P4–P5). Suppose the certified count is 60 and a new report honestly declares 61: certification fails, so the effective count remains 60. Define whether regulator intake/overlap/anomaly reports include a separately selected latest reported snapshot, and how rejected, superseded and concurrent candidates contribute. Also state whether institutional certification means attesting that a report is accurate, or attesting that every blocking validation passes. Without this distinction, the dashboard can hide the very excess admissions the product is meant to expose, or a builder may incorrectly let an excess report replace the compliant certified snapshot.

2. **High — What exactly may a replacement replace, and how does it map to counting locks?** Findings 2–3 define counting dimensions and complete declared replacements, but do not fix whether replacement scopes must be canonical, disjoint and identical to counting scopes, or may be smaller/overlapping sets. Specify the allowed scope key and how moving an episode between offerings/cohorts replaces its old and new representations. A wrong assumption permits overlapping effective snapshots, silent omission or double counting; locking one replacement key would not serialize other replacements consuming the same capacity. Approval changes additionally need a defined consequence when a new cap is below an already certified population: preserve historical certification with a current review signal, reject the change, or another explicit behavior. Locking alone cannot choose this business outcome.

3. **High — Which stable key carries a provisional student/episode through correction, and which identity outcomes block which actions?** Finding 1 preserves missing APAAR with a provisional key, while the data model explicitly leaves institution/local-student-ID period and reuse unspecified. Define the demo's local-ID uniqueness/reuse rule and how the next complete file addresses the same provisional episode when APAAR is added or corrected. State the synthetic certification/attestation matrix for missing, malformed, not-found, mismatch and unavailable evidence; only unresolved identity's prohibition on an identity-verified attestation is presently explicit. Otherwise a correction can create a second person, merge distinct people, retain a stale disclosure link, or be unnecessarily excluded from institutional reporting/certification. This concerns the mock contract, not real international-student eligibility.

4. **High — What binds permission actors and grants to records, especially after correction?** Findings 4–6 require authenticated student/guardian decisions and recipient-limited grants, but do not specify the synthetic subject-to-student/guardian binding or whether a grant authorizes one immutable snapshot or follows the current episode. Define that binding as server-controlled fixture/membership data, identify which authenticated recipient may redeem a grant, and decide whether a superseding revision requires a new decision. The route catalogue supplies no precise redemption contract, so a builder must currently invent how an approved verifier obtains the view and how grant revocation is rechecked for artifact downloads. Wrong assumptions can let an institution assign a consenting subject, let another verifier redeem a grant, or expose newly corrected fields under an earlier approval. These decisions are needed for the synthetic flow even though production identity assurance remains deferred.

## Bounded verification after revision

Re-read updated Findings 3–6 on 28 October 2026. All four findings above are resolved for the stated synthetic prototype:

- Separate reported/quality-certified pointers expose failed-certification excesses and distinguish row count from reliable headcount.
- Canonical disjoint replacement keys, affected-scope locks, linked moves and approval-decrease review define the required population changes.
- Non-reused institutional local IDs retain stable episode identity; explicit remapping and the mock outcome matrix define correction and certification behavior.
- Server-controlled synthetic OIDC bindings and snapshot/recipient-bound grants define disclosure redemption, per-download checks and supersession invalidation.

No remaining material contradiction found within this bounded review. The earlier numbered findings remain as the review record, not open questions.
