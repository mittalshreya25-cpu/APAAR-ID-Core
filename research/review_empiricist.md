## Challenge and resolution

The strongest attempted falsification was whether a successful W3C/JWT cryptographic check could be treated as a production enrolment truth/authority check. The original VCDM expressly separates truth and relying-party policy; the RFC constrains token validation without supplying issuer governance. The brief's central separation survives. The author corrected the VCDM locators; independent comparison of the revised brief with the originals already inspected resolves this objection.

The second challenge was whether suppression and evaluation prescriptions were being passed off as universal standards. NIST supports the risks and evaluation principles, but neither fixes the proposed five-person threshold, 60-second freshness, row counts, latency, or accuracy targets. The brief labels these as unmeasured engineering proposals. Preserve that label in the final research plan and avoid claiming finite adversarial cases establish universal privacy/security.

All six core factual claims are supported within the stated boundaries. Following the author's response, I independently re-read the revised EMP-2, EMP-4, EMP-6 and response paragraph and compared them with the originals already inspected. The VCDM locators and NIST publication month now match the evidence; the JWT issuer condition is explicit. Both metadata/locator objections are resolved. The existing voluntary-guidance, adopted-format, issuer-governance, caching and unmeasured-target limits remain. This is non-author source/wording verification, not product testing or legal approval. No quotation is reproduced in this review.
